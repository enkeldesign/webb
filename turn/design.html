Living reference

Built from the game, not beside it.

TURN’s interface now has a stable production language: warm paper, hard black structure, saturated categorical colour, big readable type, tactile offset shadows and short purposeful motion. This reference documents what the shipped game actually does today instead of presenting an aspirational concept board.

Current baseline One system across Home, The Lot, race HUD, dialogs and Trophy Road.
Production components are the source of truth.
Colour reinforces meaning; text and shape still carry it.
Touch, keyboard, screen reader and reduced-motion states are first-class.
1

Paper + ink

Paper is the default information surface. Ink creates borders, hierarchy and shadows. The world can be visually rich; UI surfaces stay deliberately legible.

2

Colour has a job

Pink moves the player forward, orange changes context, green confirms or rewards, blue informs, yellow warns or powers, red marks danger.

3

Interaction has weight

4px borders, rounded shapes and offset shadows make controls feel physical. Pressed states collapse that shadow instead of relying on subtle opacity.

4

Information stays literal

Difficulty, progression state, scoring state and control meaning are always named. Colour is supportive, never the only carrier of meaning.

5

Motion earns its place

Short wiggles, score pulses and control movement acknowledge state changes. Continuous motion is reserved for gameplay feedback and has a reduced-motion path.

6

Dense, not tiny

TURN can show a lot at once, especially in landscape, but touch targets stay large and high-priority information keeps strong type and spacing.

Foundations

The parts everything else is made from.

The palette is intentionally small. Primitive colours describe the visual material; semantic roles describe why a colour is being used.

Type

System sans, very heavy

Use the platform/system sans stack. Major headings and controls typically sit between 900 and 1000 weight, with tight heading tracking and uppercase control labels.

Structure

Black first

Most important component boundaries are 4px black. Large dialog and shell edges may rise to 6px. Interior dividers can step down to 2–3px.

Elevation

Offset, never blurry

Compact 3px, standard 7px and hero 12px shadows are hard black offsets. They create tactile hierarchy without soft visual noise.

Components

Controls should look like they belong in the same car.

The system is deliberately repetitive: black border, obvious fill, heavy label, readable focus, tangible pressed state. Variation comes from meaning, not decoration.

ActionsProduction hierarchy

Primary action is pink and pill-shaped. Neutral utilities recede to Paper. Navigation/context change is orange. Close is the same navigation colour even when expressed as a round icon.

Gameplay

The race HUD is instrumentation, not a website.

Gameplay UI is dense, peripheral and fast to parse. Controls preserve large target areas; feedback stays near the thing it describes and avoids blocking the road.

Lock

Unified drive pad

DRIFT, BOOST, GAS and stopping-only BRAKE share one physical block. LOCK, SHIFT and REVERSE slide out contextually rather than becoming permanent extra buttons.

Scorekeeper paper

DRIFT and FLOW use fixed-height Paper rows with attached horizontal gauges. Numbers update on a throttled presentation path; scoring logic does not depend on HUD visibility.

Feedback hierarchy

Loss, bank, multiplier, SHIFT roll and achievement feedback are brief overlays. They should never outrank steering, pace notes or immediate race safety information.

One-handed and mirrored play

The control cluster and utility group can mirror for left-handed play. Device steering keeps the mirrored driving actions without inventing an unnecessary steering pad.

Progression

Trophy Road is a literal road now.

START → every real reward in progression order → FINISH. Reward type and lock state are visible at a glance, while the label and trophy target remain the accessible source of truth.

Selection

Reward details use a modal

Do not push the selected reward’s detail panel below the fold. Place the modal above or below the selected road row according to available space; clicking outside also closes it.

Navigation

Orange closes context

Trophy Road’s detail close button uses --turn-orange-500, matching the global navigation/context-change role.

Perks

Motion means availability

Locked perks stay still. Unlocked perks may get the attention wiggle, and selecting a car with an active perk gives the short confirmation wiggle.

Accessibility

Not a mode. Part of the control system.

TURN’s accessibility work is integrated into the same product language. The visual system should never create a second-class route for non-visual, keyboard or reduced-motion use.

Screen reader

Controls expose real names, roles and states. Gesture instructions are spoken where needed. Decorative score and progress colour never replaces programmatic labels.

Drive By Ear

Audio guidance is an equal gameplay channel. Visual feedback must not steal timing or attention from pace notes, off-road direction or recovery guidance.

Blank screen

Non-visual play is a deliberate feature, not a hidden debug state. UI flows and achievement logic continue to work without the visual game layer.

Reduced motion

Wiggles, pulses and continuous score noise stop or simplify under prefers-reduced-motion. State change remains legible through colour, text and layout.

Left-handed layout

The drive pad, LOCK/SHIFT bubbles and utility group mirror spatially while preserving their labels and interaction semantics.

Focus and native semantics

Blue focus rings stay obvious. Prefer native buttons, fieldsets, radio buttons, checkboxes, details and summary before inventing a custom interaction role.

Legacy and migration

Support old selectors. Don’t design new work around them.

TURN grew quickly. Compatibility aliases and some historical class names remain in production because stability is more important than cosmetic purity.

Production reference, not proposal language

New work should start from --turn-* primitives, semantic roles, the current dialog system, unified drive pad, ScoreFeedback and the current Home/Lot/Trophy Road patterns. Old --m8-* and short aliases remain only to keep shipped components safe while they are touched naturally.

Stable system
Do

Refactor when work reaches the component

Move a touched component toward canonical tokens and anatomy when the change is already justified.

Do not

Churn working UI for purity

Do not rewrite stable gameplay or accessibility behavior merely to remove an alias or old selector name.

Regression

Pin meaning, not screenshots alone

Tests should protect semantic role, hierarchy, accessible state and critical geometry so the design system can evolve without becoming brittle.
